# Programme, staffing and budget framework

Revision 0.1 | 7 September 2026 | Planning estimates, not supplier quotations

## Recommended sequence

Research covers the full portfolio now. Develop requirements for drum brakes and disc calipers in parallel, with one named lead per platform if both progress to detailed design. Start shared inspection, traceability and advisory monitoring work immediately. Buying proven actuation and friction components reduces the number of unknowns in initial prototypes. Develop proprietary actuators and hydraulic units later where measured customer benefit supports the investment.

A programme covering every size and every industry simultaneously would multiply test configurations. The goal is a reusable design system with controlled product variants, each supported by evidence. Family coverage is not market share.

| Stage | Indicative elapsed time | Deliverable / exit gate |
|---|---|---|
| Evidence and requirements | Weeks 1-6 | Customer application matrix; engineer-approved requirements; supplier/test RFQs |
| Baseline engineering | Months 2-4 | Reviewed calculations, first CAD assemblies, material candidates, hazard analysis and test plan |
| Prototype manufacture | Months 4-7 | Supplier first articles, incoming inspection, assembled specimens and calibrated test channels |
| Bench validation and redesign | Months 6-12 | Static/dynamic/thermal data, model correlation, corrective revisions and endurance work |
| Controlled customer qualification | Months 12-24+ | Application-specific qualification, service instructions and field evidence |
| Broader portfolio | Subsequent gated releases | Additional sizes and industries supported by similarity assessment or new tests |

These stages overlap. Tooling, test-rig availability, material lead times, failures and destination-market requirements can extend them. No promised certification date is attached to this estimate.

## People and responsibilities

- Founder: customer access, supplier introductions, commercial priorities, budget and publication decisions.
- Mechanical/brake lead (existing engineer): requirements, load paths, material selection, design review and technical release authority.
- Additional mechanical designer/analyst: CAD, drawings, tolerance studies and simulation under the lead's review; dedicated second lead if both platforms advance together.
- Test/metrology engineer: fixture requirements, uncertainty, data acquisition, test execution and correlation.
- Embedded/controls engineer: sensor integration, firmware, diagnostics and machine interfaces; can start with a contract assignment.
- Supplier quality/assembly technician: incoming inspection, build records, assembly procedures and nonconformance control.
- External specialists: friction materials, spring fatigue, hydraulic design, applicable compliance and independent laboratory testing.

## Commercial evidence for the 80% question

Collect a defined sample of real RFQs and installed machines across factory cranes, ports/steel and conveyors/winches. Record product family, duty, size, quantity, annual replacement spend, current brand, delivery pain and qualification needs. Measure coverage as the share of that sample's qualified demand a proposed family can meet; show both unit-weighted and spend-weighted results. Do not extrapolate to 80% of the global market without a representative dataset.

Interview maintenance, procurement and machine engineering separately. Test willingness to pay for shorter downtime, easier adjustment, traceable inspection and diagnostic accuracy. Benchmark claims with comparable duty, friction pairs and test methods; a lower price or a sensor alone is not proof of superiority.

## First ten working days after this release

1. Engineer reviews the handbook, existing tool formats and three representative applications.
2. Founder obtains customer operating data and confidential supplier capability/quote inputs.
3. Team records missing requirements and selects first specimen configurations.
4. Test engineer requests laboratory capacity and quotation against those configurations.
5. Mechanical lead approves baseline assumptions before parametric CAD generation begins.
